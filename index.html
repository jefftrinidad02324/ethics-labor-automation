01 / THE PROBLEM

When jobs change, who carries the risk?

Automation can improve productivity, consistency, and service quality. Yet employees may face uncertain responsibilities, skills obsolescence, reduced hours, or job displacement. The ethical question is not whether innovation should stop, but whether its benefits and transition costs are distributed fairly.

Our research focuses on Philippine IT-BPO workplaces and asks how employers can provide early notice, worker consultation, protected learning time, competency-based retraining, and realistic redeployment opportunities.

Important distinction

Exposure to AI describes tasks that technology may affect. It is not a measurement of layoffs or a prediction that exposed jobs will disappear.

03 / ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

Why can workers be left behind?

Fishbone and Five Whys analysis in Milestone 1 points to a governance and capability gap: technology investment may move faster than workforce preparation.

Five Whys · From symptoms to root cause

  1. Workers face uncertain roles as tasks change.
  2. Retraining and redeployment may not be ready.
  3. Training is not always linked to future vacancies.
  4. Transition costs are omitted from technology decisions.
  5. Workforce preparation lacks clear early ownership.
Root cause: a gap between automation governance and worker capability planning.
04 / THE HUMAN STORY

Beyond efficiency: the ethics of change

These are illustrative situations, not interviews or verified employee testimonies.

01 · EMPLOYEE EXPERIENCE

“Will I still have a role?”

An experienced customer support agent may see routine inquiries handled by AI. Anxiety can begin long before any formal layoff. Early communication and meaningful consultation help address uncertainty.

02 · UNEQUAL ACCESS

“Can I afford to retrain?”

Workers with caregiving responsibilities, limited connectivity, or difficult schedules may be excluded by training offered outside paid working hours. Accessibility must be designed into eligibility and delivery.

03 · EMPLOYMENT QUALITY

“What if no vacancy exists?”

A certificate alone does not create a job. Responsible programs must verify suitable roles, provide transparent matching, and prepare alternative transition support when internal placement is impossible.

Think about it: Who gets a voice before automation decisions are approved, and who is accountable when promised opportunities do not materialize?

Research transparency

This is a secondary-source research and intervention-design project. It uses published reports, analytical synthesis, and researcher-scored alternatives. It does not claim original interviews, completed employer partnerships, verified participant results, or observed impacts from the proposed pilot. The ADB survey figures are historical, WEF indicators are employer expectations, and ILO exposure estimates are not forecasts of layoffs.
